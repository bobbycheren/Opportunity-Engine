# More Education: Training, Apprenticeships, College, Finishing a Degree, Graduate School

Written November 2026. Tuition, financial aid rules, award amounts, and deadlines change every year, and federal student aid rules changed in 2026. Use this file as a map, then **confirm current details with a web search** of official sources (studentaid.gov, the school's own pages, the state's college-access site, apprenticeship.gov, careeronestop.org) before anyone applies, borrows, or pays. Never quote an award amount, a loan limit, or a deadline from memory.

## 1. How to coach this pathway

1. **Start from the goal, not the program.** "What work do you want this to lead to?" A program is worth it only if it opens work they want, at pay that justifies the cost, the time, and the income given up.
2. **Find out what they already have:** high school diploma or equivalency, college credits (where, how many, how long ago), licenses, certifications, military or work training. Old credits often count.
3. **Compare two or three routes** in the plan's "Programs I'm considering" table: level, school and accreditation, what's required to get in, length and schedule, the full cost, aid, start date, and what graduates actually earn.
4. **Test first:** talk to the program and to recent graduates, ask for the job placement rate and typical starting pay, sit in on a class, take one class or a free module, and get credits evaluated before enrolling.
5. **Plan the money** (section 7) and the application steps with dates.
6. **Keep earning if possible.** Many adults do best part-time, online, in the evening, through an apprenticeship that pays, or with employer tuition help.

Use the assistance dial for choosing a program, applications and essays, and the money plan. Never apply, submit the FAFSA, sign, or pay on their behalf.

## 2. The menu of routes

| Route | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-term training and certificates** | Weeks to a year: CNA, CDL, welding, phlebotomy, IT certifications, HVAC, medical coding, and more, at community and technical colleges or approved providers | Getting into a better-paying job fast |
| **Registered apprenticeships** | A paid job with on-the-job training and classes, ending in a recognized credential (apprenticeship.gov) | Earning while learning; skilled trades and more and more other fields |
| **Associate degree** | About two years full-time at a community or technical college; career programs or transfer programs | A career credential, or a cheaper start on a bachelor's |
| **Bachelor's degree** | About four years full-time, less with transfer or prior-learning credit | Careers that require it; moving up |
| **Finishing a degree they started** | Section 4 | Some of the best returns there are, because much of the cost is already paid |
| **Graduate and professional school** | Master's, law, medicine, other professional and doctoral programs | Careers that require them; check the payoff carefully (section 5) |
| **High school equivalency and adult education** | Free or low-cost classes and the state's equivalency test | Anyone without a diploma; often the first step to everything else |

## 3. Choosing a program and a school safely

- **Accreditation:** check the school in the U.S. Department of Education's database of accredited schools or with CHEA. Unaccredited credits usually don't transfer and don't qualify for federal aid.
- **Outcomes:** College Scorecard (collegescorecard.ed.gov) shows typical earnings and debt by school and field. Ask the program directly for completion and job placement rates; a program that won't share them is a yellow flag.
- **Licensing:** for licensed jobs (nursing, cosmetology, trades, counseling, teaching), confirm the program qualifies them to sit for the license in their state.
- **Transfer:** if they may continue later, ask now which credits will transfer, and get it in writing.
- **Schedule and support:** night, weekend, online, and hybrid options; childcare, tutoring, adult-learner advising.
- **Red flags:** pressure to sign today, promises of guaranteed jobs, recruiters paid per enrollment, vague costs, "free laptop" offers, programs that cost far more than the same credential at a community college.

## 4. Finishing a degree they started

Millions of adults have some college and no degree. Their credits are an asset.

1. **Get their transcripts** from each school (often free or cheap online). If an unpaid balance is holding a transcript, ask the school about payment plans and their rules; federal rules and some state laws limit when schools can withhold transcripts.
2. **Get a credit evaluation** from two or three schools before choosing. Programs built for adults returning to finish (many states have a degree-completion bachelor's for adults) often accept more credits and credit for experience.
3. **Credit for what they already know:** prior learning assessment (a portfolio of work experience), exams such as CLEP and DSST, credit for military training (the Joint Services Transcript) and some work training and licenses.
4. **Check aid eligibility:** old failing grades or withdrawals can affect satisfactory academic progress, which can be appealed. Defaulted federal loans block new federal aid, but loan rehabilitation or consolidation can restore eligibility; point them to studentaid.gov and their loan servicer, and to the financial aid office.
5. **Size it:** how many credits remain, how many classes per term fit their life, and the realistic finish date.

## 5. Graduate and professional school

- **Start from the job.** Does the target career require the degree, or just reward it? Look at real postings and at what people in the role actually hold.
- **Compare the full cost with the payoff:** tuition, fees, and years of reduced earnings against the realistic pay difference, from real sources.
- **Funding:** employer tuition help, assistantships and fellowships that cover tuition, scholarships, and only then loans. Federal graduate loan rules changed in 2026, with new limits; check studentaid.gov for the current rules before planning on loans. Public service jobs may qualify for loan forgiveness programs; verify the current rules.
- **Admissions:** tests where required (some programs have dropped them), a statement of purpose, letters of recommendation, and for some fields prior coursework. Help them build a timeline backward from deadlines.
- **Test first:** talk to two people in the field who hold the degree and two who don't.

## 6. Applications

- Build a timeline backward from each deadline: transcripts, recommendations, tests, essays, the FAFSA, scholarship applications.
- Essays and statements: use the assistance dial. Their story, their voice, their facts. Never invent or inflate.
- Keep every application, login reminder (not passwords), and deadline in the plan's application tracker.

## 7. Paying for it

- **The FAFSA** (studentaid.gov) is the door to federal grants, work-study, federal loans, and most state and school aid. Filing is free. Never pay anyone to file it. Each person needs their own FSA ID; never ask for or store theirs.
- **Pell Grants** are need-based federal grants that don't have to be repaid, with a lifetime limit. **Workforce Pell,** a newer federal program, can cover some short job-training programs; check whether a given program qualifies.
- **State grants and programs.** Many states have grants for adults, free community college for high-demand fields, or adult degree-completion programs. Look these up on the state's college-access site and confirm the rules and deadlines.
- **Workforce funding:** American Job Centers can fund training for eligible people through the federal workforce system, for programs on the state's approved training provider list. Ask the local center.
- **Employer tuition help:** many employers pay for classes; up to a set amount each year can be tax-free to the employee under federal law. Ask HR, and check any repayment clause before signing.
- **Military and veterans:** GI Bill benefits through the VA; tuition assistance for those serving.
- **Scholarships:** the school's own, local foundations, CareerOneStop's scholarship finder. Never pay to apply for a scholarship.
- **Loans last:** federal loans before private loans, because federal loans have income-driven repayment and other protections. Model the monthly payment against realistic pay before borrowing. This is a "stop and check with a person" decision.
- **Total cost, not tuition:** fees, books, tools, uniforms, testing and licensing fees, transportation, childcare, and the income given up.

## 8. West Virginia notes

Verify each before relying on it; programs and rules change.
- **College Foundation of West Virginia (cfwv.com):** the state's college-access site, with aid programs, applications, and planning tools.
- **WV Invests Grant:** a state program that has covered tuition for certain high-demand certificate and associate programs at community and technical colleges for eligible residents, with conditions. Check current eligibility and requirements.
- **Regents Bachelor of Arts (RBA):** a degree-completion program for adults at West Virginia public colleges that has given credit for prior learning and experience. Check which schools offer it now and its current rules.
- **WV Higher Education Grant:** a need-based state grant; check eligibility and deadlines.
- **WorkForce West Virginia** runs the state's American Job Centers, the door to federally funded training.

## 9. Education scams and pitfalls

- Diploma mills and "degrees" based on a fee and a resume. Check accreditation.
- Anyone charging to file the FAFSA, find aid, or "guarantee" scholarships.
- Student-debt "relief" companies that charge for free federal programs.
- High-pressure recruiters and enrollment agreements signed the same day.
- Private loans offered before federal aid is used.
- Online "certificates" employers don't recognize. Useful for learning; be honest that they aren't credentials.

## 10. Where to get human help

The school's admissions and financial aid offices, a community college adviser, adult-learner or veteran services offices, the local American Job Center, the state's college-access program, apprenticeship sponsors and union training centers, and the employer's HR office for tuition help. Offer to draft their questions and rehearse the call.
