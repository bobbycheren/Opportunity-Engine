# Refugees in Kakuma and Kalobeyei: Jobs, Business, and Education

Written October 2026. Read this whenever someone is in Kakuma or Kalobeyei, or mentions refugee documents, movement passes, UNHCR, or DRS (the Department of Refugee Services). The rules for refugees are different, unclear in places, and changing. **Never guess.** When a step depends on documents or status, send them to the agencies below and help them prepare their questions.

PFC runs the Mapato Sewing School in Kakuma, so many users here will be its graduates, and some computer-school graduates may be refugees too. Some sewing graduates have pooled money to buy machines and work together in a shared workshop. All three pathways (job, business, education) are possible for refugees, but each has extra steps and uncertainties. The coach should be honest about both.

## Respect and privacy first

- Never ask for nationality, ethnicity, a refugee ID or case number, manifest number, or resettlement status. If a step needs a document, ask only: "Do you have the document this step needs?"
- Don't assume grid power, a private phone, a bank account, English, or freedom to travel outside Turkana County.
- Many people in Kakuma have been through hard things. Be patient, plain, and kind, and let them lead.
- Answer in the language they use when you can (often Kiswahili, French, Arabic, Somali, English, or others).

## The legal picture (as far as is known)

- **Right to work and do business.** The Refugees Act 2021 (section 28) gives recognised refugees the right to work, run businesses, and practise professions, subject to the law. Don't say refugees can't legally work, and don't say they can work freely without a permit. In practice, paperwork and offices are the barrier.
- **Class M work permit.** This is the free immigration permit for recognised refugees who work or do business (apply online at fns.immigration.go.ke; Form 25, a cover letter from the employer or self, a photo, the refugee certificate, and a recognition letter from UNHCR and the Department of Refugee Services). The government said in November 2025 that refugees "are being issued" Class M permits, but NGOs reported in March 2026 that only about 100 a year are issued, processing takes 3 to 24 months, permits are tied to one employer and role, and applicants are often asked for documents most refugees can't get (a passport, a tax compliance certificate). It is **unclear whether a self-employed tailor in the camp needs one**. Don't tell anyone they must or must not have it; refer them to UNHCR, Refugee Consortium of Kenya (RCK), or NRC's legal aid (ICLA), which help with applications.
- **Business permit.** The county business permit is what officials usually check for camp businesses. Turkana County permits are renewed every January. The Biashara Centre in Kakuma (at Kabogorit) handles permits, registration, and business support. Current fees for Kakuma traders aren't published in a reliable source: tell them to ask at the Biashara Centre or the county revenue office, pay only through the official system, and keep the official receipt. Fake permit brokers have operated in the past. Having a permit can make other things easier, such as movement passes and loans.
- **Movement passes: reports conflict.** In November 2025 the government said movement passes had been scrapped as part of turning the camps into settlements (Kenyans.co.ke, 27 Nov 2025), but a 2026 field report found DRS still requiring passes in practice to leave Kakuma, with business passes of about 14 days and some traders refused without a business certificate. UNHCR's help pages have listed self-booking at Field Posts 1, 2, and 4 and in Kalobeyei on weekdays. Before anyone travels (for example to buy fabric in Kitale or Lodwar), tell them the rules are unclear and to confirm with DRS or UNHCR (1517). Never suggest travelling without whatever document DRS requires, and never suggest paying anyone to speed one up.
- **The Shirika Plan** (launched March 2025) aims to turn Kakuma and Kalobeyei into integrated settlements under county government. As of 2026 daily life and business rules have changed little, refugees remain largely limited to the host county, and the plan isn't fully in law. Don't describe it as done or say refugees have full freedom of movement.

## Job pathway for refugees

- **Formal jobs** with companies or organisations usually need a Class M permit, which is slow and scarce (above). Help them prepare a strong CV and portfolio anyway, and point them to UNHCR, RCK, or NRC for the permit.
- **Incentive work** (stipended roles with NGOs and agencies in the camp) is a common form of work. Current rates and rules weren't verifiable; ask the agency.
- **Local hiring pressure:** Turkana County leaders have said Shirika jobs should go first to Turkana youth. Be realistic.
- **Online and digital work:** possible, but platforms often block refugees at ID verification, many can't receive payments, and connectivity in Kakuma is weak and shared. Paid work has come mostly through referrals from training organisations. AI and data "gig" tasks have paid in discretionary rewards rather than wages, and refugee transcription and data jobs have shrunk. Don't present gig platforms as reliable income, and never suggest borrowing or renting someone else's account or ID.
- **Overseas labour mobility:** a few genuine programmes exist (for example RefugePoint's, posted by UNHCR in September 2026, for recognised refugees aged 21–55 with experience in IT, electrical work, or nursing). Legitimate programmes are free and announced on help.unhcr.org/kenya/announcements. UNHCR's skills registration does not guarantee placement. Anyone charging money is a scammer.

## Education and training pathway for refugees

- **KUCCPS:** refugees who completed KCSE can apply for university and TVET placement through KUCCPS (per UNHCR).
- **Funding:** HELB loans and government scholarships appear to be for Kenyan citizens (they require a national ID); no change for refugees was found as of October 2026. Whether refugees pay Kenyan or international fees at public institutions couldn't be verified: tell them to ask the institution. In practice, most refugees study through **scholarships**.
- **Scholarships** (check each for a current call; all genuine ones are free to apply; never pay anyone):
  - **DAFI** (UNHCR, run with Windle International Kenya): for qualified refugees in Kenya; calls have opened once a year, around April–May. The last official Kenya call found was posted 9 May 2024; no 2025 or 2026 call was found. Official pages list no grade or age rule (don't quote secondary sources as fact). Ask Windle International Kenya's Kakuma office and check help.unhcr.org/kenya.
  - **WUSC Student Refugee Program** (study in Canada): calls around February (the 2027–28 call ran 13 February–13 March 2026); ages 18–25 on 1 January of the call year; single with no children; living in Kenya continuously for three years; KCSE B+ (B- for female applicants); original UNHCR proof of registration and Kenyan refugee ID; original school certificates and result slips; good English; and eligible for Canada's private-sponsorship resettlement. State these criteria and let the person judge for themselves; never ask about family status or resettlement eligibility. Free to apply.
  - **Mastercard Foundation Scholars at USIU-Africa** (Nairobi): full cost; minimum C+; refugees up to age 32 (per USIU); enquiries to mcfsp-enquiries@usiu.ac.ke. Studying in Nairobi needs a DRS travel document; plan early.
  - **Online degrees:** Jesuit Worldwide Learning (JWL) in Kakuma, with online bachelor's study through partner universities after JWL's own certificate courses; the SLU bachelor's needs a JWL certificate and English at B2, and JWL provides computer labs, so no laptop is needed. JRS Kenya with SNHU ran a call 16–31 March 2026 (laptop and data provided; appears Nairobi-based; check eligibility for Kakuma). Check intakes and device and connectivity needs.
  - New calls are posted on **help.unhcr.org/kenya/announcements**.
- **Vocational training in Kakuma:** Don Bosco Technical Institute Kakuma (tailoring, computer, electrical and solar, and other trades; ask which body certifies each course), other camp training centres, and newer institutes such as Namelon Training Institute (check courses, fees, and eligibility).
- **Recognition of Prior Learning has been done for refugees in Kakuma**, including in tailoring (assessed by a panel with trainers from Kitale National Polytechnic, using a portfolio of photos, videos, and testimonials, even without formal documents). Ask training partners whether a round is planned.
- **NITA trade tests:** access for refugees wasn't verified; ask NITA or a training centre.
- **KCSE as a private candidate:** registration accepts a national ID, passport, or alien card; whether a refugee ID is accepted wasn't confirmed. Ask the Sub-County Director of Education or KNEC.
- **Recognition of home-country qualifications:** through KNQA (equation of foreign qualifications; about KSh 10,000 and 14 working days in its 2024 charter; no refugee waiver found) or the Commission for University Education for degrees.
- **Studying outside the camp** may need a DRS travel document (see movement passes above). A 2026 report cites a DRS guideline of about four months for education passes; delays have cost people scholarships and interviews. For any exam, interview, or course outside the camp, ask DRS or UNHCR (1517) as soon as the date is known.

## Money and identity

| Item | What we know (October 2026) |
|---|---|
| SIM card and M-Pesa | Refugees can register SIM cards in their own name with a refugee ID (since 2025). Borrowed-ID SIMs are no longer allowed. Expired IDs can freeze mobile money. |
| Pochi la Biashara | Opens from an M-Pesa-registered line with no paperwork; not specifically confirmed for refugee-ID lines, so suggest they try or ask at a Safaricom shop. |
| KRA PIN | Possible but conditional. For refugees in business, KRA has listed: refugee ID, a DRS introduction letter, a business registration certificate, and a tax agent's introduction letter with the agent's PIN and compliance certificate (employed refugees use a Class M permit and employer letter instead). UNHCR says business registration needs a KRA PIN, and the county permit needs a registration certificate, so each paper can depend on another. Don't try to untangle this alone: UNHCR, RCK, NRC, and the Biashara Centre help with it. |
| Bank accounts | Possible but uneven. Equity Bank and KCB have branches in Kakuma, and some banks accept new-generation refugee IDs. Older IDs can't always be verified. |
| Hustler Fund | **Not available.** It requires a Kenyan national ID. |
| NYOTA | Places were reserved for refugee and host-community youth in its business grants. Check whether an intake is open and who is eligible. |
| Savings groups | Widespread and accessible. Community savings and credit groups of 15–30 members, often with a three-key box, are common in Kakuma. Self-help groups register with the Social Development Officer (at the DCC compound in Kakuma, per UNHCR). Merry-go-rounds work the same way here (see `money-and-groups.md`). |

## Business pathway: support and finance in and around Kakuma

Check what's currently open before suggesting any of these; programmes start and stop with funding.
- **Biashara Centre (Kabogorit, Kakuma):** permits, registration, business support, and links to county youth and women funds.
- **Inkomoko:** business training and finance for refugee and host-community entrepreneurs in Kakuma and Kalobeyei; has reported lending at lower rates than banks.
- **Kakuma Kalobeyei Challenge Fund (KKCF):** IFC-backed fund that has supported local businesses, including a tailoring business focused on school uniforms. No grant window was confirmed open in 2026.
- **Banks:** Equity Bank and KCB. Bank loans in Kakuma have been around 20% interest.
- **Microfinance and SACCOs:** for example RAFODE and Kenya Bankers Sacco have operated in Kakuma.
- **NGO livelihood programmes** (for example LWF, NRC, DRC, IRC, ILO): these have supported tailors with training, machines, uniform tenders, and grants in the past. Ask locally what's running.

## Market realities

- **Demand is squeezed.** Food aid cuts in 2025 reduced how much money many households have, and small businesses in the camp felt it. Test demand carefully and keep stock low.
- **School uniforms, repairs, and occasion clothing** are the established tailoring markets. Selling to the Turkana host community and to Kakuma town is realistic.
- **Supplies** often come from distant towns, which means transport costs and a movement pass. Buying together as a group can cut costs.
- **Power:** some areas have solar mini-grid power, often not around the clock. Manual or treadle machines avoid the problem.

## Protection contacts

- **UNHCR Help Line: 1517** (free, 24 hours; confirmed on UNHCR's Kenya help site). Also for reporting fraud or exploitation.
- National numbers: **1195** gender-based violence hotline · **116** Childline Kenya · **999 / 112** police. Confirm before relying on them.

## What to tell people about fraud

- All UNHCR services, including registration and resettlement, are free, and so are genuine scholarships and labour-mobility programmes. Resettlement is never guaranteed and can't be bought. Anyone asking for money or favours for a UNHCR service is committing fraud; report it to 1517.
- Business success, payments, or contacts have no effect on resettlement. Never imply otherwise.
- **Scholarship scams:** "guaranteed" scholarships abroad, "processing fees," or requests for a photo of an ID are fraud. Don't pay or send documents; screenshot; report to 1517, the platform, and the real scholarship provider.
- **Renting or borrowing a verified freelancing account:** explain why it's unsafe: payments go to the owner, platforms close shared accounts and can hold earnings, and it means using someone else's identity. Offer the safe route: build skills and a small portfolio, use only accounts in their own name, and look for referral work through training organisations.
- If someone offers "faster resettlement" or any UNHCR help for money: don't pay, don't share a case number or documents, don't confront the person, keep any messages, and report to 1517. If money was already sent by M-Pesa, also call Safaricom about the transaction.

## Things the coach must not do

- Quote permit fees, tax rules, or loan terms for Kakuma as fact.
- Suggest the Hustler Fund, a borrowed ID, a borrowed SIM, or someone else's M-Pesa.
- Suggest travel without checking with DRS what document is needed, or paying anyone to speed up a pass or permit.
- Promise grants, scholarships, permits, resettlement, overseas jobs, or programmes that haven't been confirmed open.
- Assume HELB, government scholarships, or Kenyan fee rates apply to refugees.
- Present online gig platforms as reliable income.
- Suggest misstating nationality or status, or travelling on a home-country passport; this can put refugee status at risk.
- Encourage borrowing without a clear repayment plan; many households are already in debt.
