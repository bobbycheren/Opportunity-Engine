# Kenya: The Job Market

Written October 2026. Wages, fees, and programmes change. **Confirm figures with a web search before relying on them**, and always give users the date and source of any number.

## 1. The big picture (be honest about it)

- Most work in Kenya is informal. In 2025 there were about 18 million informal jobs against about 3.3 million formal wage jobs, and roughly nine in ten new jobs were informal (KNBS Economic Survey 2026).
- Youth face the hardest market. In 2022, about 27.5% of Kenyans aged 20–24 were not in employment, education, or training (KNBS). A 2026 report found only about 8.6% of employed Kenyan youth were in formal jobs.
- That does not mean "there are no jobs." It means: (1) formal jobs are competitive, so a good CV, the right documents, and persistence matter; (2) skills plus a recognised certificate improve the odds; (3) self-employment and informal work are real paths, and can be stepping stones.
- Growing areas for young people: business process outsourcing (BPO) and call centres, digital and online work, construction, finance, hospitality and trade, logistics and delivery, health care, agriculture value chains, and skilled trades.

Coach honestly: don't promise a salaried job; help them build the strongest realistic plan, often a mix of training, a first job or attachment, and side income.

## 2. Pay

**Statutory minimum wages (General Wages Order, effective 1 May 2026; Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Eldoret), per month:**

| Job | KSh |
|---|---|
| Cleaner, messenger, watchman | 18,047 |
| Night watchman | 20,134 |
| Junior clerk, petrol attendant | 23,350 |
| Copy typist, light van driver | 24,359 |
| General clerk, receptionist, telephone operator | 27,797 |
| Salesperson | 33,820 |
| Cashier | 40,724 |

Former municipalities and other areas have lower schedules. Trade unions have disputed whether the 2026 order delivered the full announced increase, so check the current figures. Many informal jobs pay below these rates. Help users know their rights, but also be realistic about the market.

**Rough entry-level ranges (not authoritative):** call-centre agent in Nairobi about KSh 25,000–40,000 a month; customer service roles often around KSh 20,000–30,000. Search current adverts for real figures in the user's town.

**What entry jobs ask for:** requirements vary. Some BPO and call-centre adverts ask only for a KCSE certificate (e.g., a CCI Global advert on Fuzu, September 2026); others want a diploma and experience. Most test spoken English and typing; ICDL is rarely listed. Check real adverts before saying more training is needed.

**Deductions to explain on a formal payslip:** PAYE income tax; NSSF (6% employee from February 2026, on earnings between KSh 9,000 and KSh 108,000); SHA health contribution (2.75%); housing levy (1.5%). At low wages PAYE is often zero after personal relief. Example: at KSh 25,000 gross, PAYE is roughly nil after personal relief and take-home is about KSh 22,400 (verify with a current PAYE calculator). The first salary usually comes about a month after starting, so plan fare, lunch, and childcare for that first month. Help users estimate take-home pay, not just gross.

## 3. Where to look for jobs

- **Job boards:** BrighterMonday (brightermonday.co.ke), MyJobMag (myjobmag.co.ke), Fuzu (fuzu.com), Corporate Staffing Services (corporatestaffing.co.ke), LinkedIn.
- **National Employment Authority (NEAIMS):** the government jobs and recruitment system, with local and overseas jobs and licensed agencies. Register with your national ID. Toll-free 0800 222 223.
- **Government jobs:** Public Service Commission portal (publicservice.go.ke; jobs at pscims.publicservice.go.ke) and each County Public Service Board's website.
- **BPO and call-centre employers** recruit in large numbers, especially in Nairobi. Search for current openings.
- **Offline:** most entry jobs in Kenya are still found through people. Encourage telling relatives, church members, former teachers, and neighbours what work they are looking for; visiting businesses in person with a printed CV; and checking local notice boards. Help them prepare a short, polite introduction.
- **Digital hubs and county youth offices** often share opportunities.

## 4. Documents employers commonly ask for

| Document | How to get it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| National ID | Registration of Persons office | First-time ID is free. Needed for nearly everything. |
| KRA PIN | itax.kra.go.ke, using the ID | Free. Needed for formal jobs, HELB, and online-income tax. |
| Certificate of Good Conduct (police clearance) | Apply on eCitizen (DCI); fingerprints at a Huduma Centre or DCI | About KSh 1,050. A new system launched in 2026 speeds processing. Employers usually want a recent one. |
| SHA registration (replaced NHIF) | USSD *147#, the SHA portal, or a Huduma Centre | Required for medical cover. |
| NSSF number | Usually registered by the employer | Social security. |
| HELB clearance certificate | HELB website or eCitizen | Needed for many public-sector jobs. Free for people who never had a HELB loan. |
| Academic certificates and result slips | School, KNEC, college | Keep copies; certified copies are often requested. |

A good early step for many users: get the ID, KRA PIN, and SHA sorted, and know how to get a Certificate of Good Conduct when an employer asks.

## 5. Scams (warn early and often)

Common patterns in Kenya in 2025–26:
- Fake job adverts using government or big-company logos (SHA, Kenya Railways, National Police Service, and others).
- Requests for "interview," "uniform," "training," "processing," or "medical" fees.
- Staged interviews in hotels where applicants are robbed.
- Sale of fake recruitment letters (for example, for police or the military).
- Fake M-Pesa "reversal" messages.
- "Work online and earn" schemes that require paying first, renting or buying "verified" accounts on freelance platforms, or pay-to-unlock task apps.
- Pyramid and "high-return" investment schemes.

**The rule:** genuine employers do not charge job seekers money. Check the employer's official website and phone number independently. Never share M-Pesa PINs or one-time codes.

## 6. Overseas jobs

- Kenya has labour agreements with several countries, including the UK, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Germany, Austria, and Italy. Hundreds of thousands of Kenyans have been placed abroad.
- **Before anything else, do NEA's two checks on NEAIMS:** (1) the recruitment agency is registered and licensed; (2) the specific job is genuine. Over 600 agencies have been deregistered for malpractice.
- By regulation, licensed agencies charge their service fee to the employer. A job seeker may only be charged limited administrative costs (such as trade tests), capped at one month of the proposed salary, and **only by a NEAIMS-licensed agency, for a NEAIMS-listed job, under a written contract.** Anyone else asking for money (a WhatsApp contact, a "friend of a friend," an unlicensed office) is a red flag, whatever the amount. The contract must say who pays the visa, airfare, and medical. New legislation was still before Parliament in 2026 *(in transition)*.
- Never travel on a visit or tourist visa to take a job. Never hand a passport to an agent or employer to keep. Save the Kenyan embassy contacts for the destination country before leaving.
- The government has warned about trafficking into exploitative work, recruitment into foreign wars, and scam compounds in Southeast Asia. If an offer is vague about the employer, country, or job, or rushes the person to pay, treat it as a red flag.

## 7. Online and digital work

- Real paths include virtual assistance, data entry and annotation, transcription, customer support, digital marketing, graphic design, writing, and BPO work. Skills usually matter more than certificates, and a small portfolio of sample work helps.
- Free training: Ajira Digital and Jitume hubs (see `education-and-training.md`).
- Online income is taxable. A KRA PIN is needed (for refugees the PIN is conditional; see `refugees.md`), and platforms such as YouTube now withhold Kenyan tax on earnings.
- Warn against account renting, paying for "tasks," and any platform that asks for money before work.
- Be honest: building online income takes time, good internet, and reliable payment methods. It works best alongside other income at first.

## 8. Jobs using sewing skills

For sewing-school graduates who want a wage rather than (or alongside) their own business. Written October 2026; confirm details before advising.

**Where the jobs are**
- **Garment factories (EPZ apparel).** Kenya's export garment factories employed about 66,800 people directly in 2024 (KNBS), most of them women, mainly in **Athi River/Mavoko, Nairobi, Mombasa, and the coast (Mtwapa, Kikambala in Kilifi)**, with large local mills such as Bedi in **Nakuru**. Their orders depend on the US trade deal (AGOA), which lapsed in 2025 and was extended in 2026 to **31 December 2028**. Hiring fell during the uncertainty; firms now have a window to grow, but no specific hiring numbers had been announced. Search for current openings before anyone travels.
- **Tailoring shops and uniform makers** often pay by the piece. No reliable figures exist; help them ask several shops what they pay and how regularly.
- **Institutions:** schools, hotels, hospitals, and uniform suppliers sometimes employ tailors. Search county and company adverts.
- **Bag and leather workshops**, upholstery, and curtain makers use similar skills.

**How factories recruit**
- Some through employer-linked training: **Generation Kenya's Sewing Machine Operator programme** (8 weeks, full-time, for ages 18–35 with KCPE or higher) links graduates to factories on the coast and elsewhere and guarantees an **interview**, not a job. Classes run full days on weekdays for 8 weeks, so there are no earnings during the course; plan childcare and money. Check current intakes and any cost at kenya.generation.org.
- Some at the factory gate or through notices. There is no evidence that factories require a NITA certificate, but a trade test (see `education-and-training.md`) helps show skill.
- Crowds at factory gates have turned dangerous when rumours of jobs were false. Confirm openings first.

**Pay: know the legal floor.** The 2026 wages order (effective 1 May 2026) sets monthly minimums by job and area. Figures as summarised by Grant Thornton (Legal Notice 95 of 2026). These are basic pay; where the employer provides no housing, a housing allowance of 15% of basic is added (for example 15,428 + 2,314 = 17,742). Which column a town falls in isn't always clear (for example Mtwapa or Kikambala), so ask the employer or the county labour office before quoting a floor, and check the Gazette before relying on these:

| Category | Cities (Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Eldoret) | Former municipalities, plus Mavoko (Athi River), Ruiru and Limuru town councils | All other areas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machine attendant | KSh 20,455 | KSh 19,035 | KSh 15,428 |
| Machinist (made to measure) | KSh 23,350 | KSh 21,844 | KSh 17,859 |
| Tailor | KSh 30,635 | KSh 28,156 | KSh 25,106 |

Which category an employer uses for a factory operator varies; ask. Many factories are unionised (Tailors and Textile Workers Union), and a union agreement may pay more. Compare take-home pay after deductions, fare, rent, and lunch, especially if moving to Athi River or Mombasa.

**Conditions and rights.** Factory work usually means long days on a production line and targets. Workers have rights to overtime pay, safe conditions, and freedom from harassment; the union and the labour office can help. Don't present unverified complaints about specific factories as fact.

**A possible ladder** (general industry practice, not a documented Kenyan pathway): machine operator → multi-skilled operator → line leader or supervisor → quality control, or → sewing machine mechanic, pattern maker, or embroidery operator with further training (see `education-and-training.md`). NITA trade test grades help at each step.

**Scams.** No genuine factory charges a "registration," training, or uniform fee for a job, and no guard or "agent" at the gate may charge to put a name on a hiring list. Overseas "factory worker" or "tailor" adverts that ask for money, a passport, or travel on a tourist visa are red flags; check the agency and the job on NEAIMS.
