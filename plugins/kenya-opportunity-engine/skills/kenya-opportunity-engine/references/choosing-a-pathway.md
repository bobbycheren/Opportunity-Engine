# Choosing a Pathway: Job, Business, or Further Education

Read this whenever someone is deciding where to put their time and money, or mixing pathways. The person decides. Your job is an honest comparison with real Kenyan figures, a small test before a big commitment, and room to change course.

## The three pathways in one look

| | A job | A business | Further education or training |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best when** | They need steady income soon, want to learn from others, or want medical cover and NSSF | They have evidence people will pay, can live with irregular income, and like running things | A recognised certificate would clearly open better work, and they can manage the fees, time, and lost income |
| **Main risks** | Few formal openings; scams; low pay; fare and rent eat wages | Irregular income; selling on credit; debt; family pressure | Fees and lost earnings; unaccredited colleges; a course that doesn't lead to work |
| **Main files** | `kenya-job-market.md`, `job-search-toolkit.md` | `business-modes.md`, `tailoring-business.md`, `money-and-groups.md`, `kenya-business-rules.md` | `education-and-training.md` |

## Mixes are common, and often smartest

- **A job now, training later:** earn first, save for fees, study part-time or in a later intake.
- **A job plus a side business:** a machine operator who sews for neighbours at weekends; a data clerk who types and prints for people.
- **Training while earning:** evening or weekend classes, a trade test taken while working, online study, attachment that leads to a job.
- **A business plus a certificate:** a tailor who takes the NITA trade test to win school or tender orders; a cyber-café owner who adds ICDL.
- **Working inside a group:** a shared workshop or a group of graduates sharing machines, rent, and orders.
- **Waiting and saving first** is a real option, not a failure.

## How to compare, step by step

1. **Name two to four real options** in their words ("machine operator at a factory in Athi River," "repairs and alterations from home," "Fashion Design certificate at the nearest polytechnic").
2. **Collect real figures for each** with a web search and, where needed, local asking:
   - Income: pay in current adverts in their area, or local prices for their product, or the pay that a qualification leads to. Source and date each figure.
   - Costs: fare, rent, lunch, childcare (including sick days and early or late shifts), tools, materials, fees, exam fees, data, and cash to get through the first month before a first salary.
   - Time: when money starts coming in, and how long a course takes.
   - What it opens next: the next step up and what it pays.
3. **Do the money picture** for each: money in minus money out per month, and how many months until it pays off. A KSh 25,000 job in Nairobi with high rent and fare may leave less than KSh 18,000 near home. A KSh 67,000-a-year course also costs the income given up while studying.
4. **Weigh what matters to them** (their top three from the intake) and family duties.
5. **Name the riskiest assumption** for each option ("Factories near me are hiring," "Parents will order uniforms from me," "A certificate will get me a better job").
6. **Design a small test** for the favourite (see below), then decide.

Put the comparison in the plan's "Paths I'm comparing" table. In chat, give each path in two or three short lines unless they ask for a table.

## Small tests before big commitments

| Pathway | Example tests |
|---|---|
| Job | Talk to two people doing the work; visit a factory gate or office on a hiring day; apply to five real adverts and see what responses come |
| Business | Ask ten potential customers; take three orders with deposits; offer repairs for two weeks with a small spending cap |
| Education | Visit the college and talk to current students; check accreditation and which body certifies the course; ask graduates whether it led to work; try a free module or a short course first |

Set a spending cap and a deadline, and ask them to set what success looks like. After the test, review honestly: continue, adjust, or switch.

## For computer-school graduates

Realistic starting directions:
- **Job:** customer care and BPO, data entry, office assistant, cyber café attendant, shop or till work using computers, ICT support for experienced users, online work (with care about scams).
- **Business:** cyber services (typing, printing, online applications for others), small online selling, M-Pesa agency, a service using their existing skills.
- **Further education:** ICDL for a recognised computer credential; TVET ICT courses (artisan, craft certificate, diploma) by KCSE grade; free digital-skills programmes; degree routes for C+ and above or via a diploma.

## For sewing-school graduates

Realistic starting directions:
- **Job:** machine operator in a garment factory, piece work for an established tailor or uniform maker, a tailoring job at a school, hotel, or institution, or work in a bag or leather workshop.
- **Business:** repairs and alterations, school uniforms, made-to-order outfits, bags, a group workshop (see `tailoring-business.md`).
- **Further education:** NITA trade tests in dressmaking or garment making, Recognition of Prior Learning, TVET Fashion Design and Garment Making courses, short courses in pattern making or industrial machines (see `education-and-training.md`).

## For refugees in Kakuma and Kalobeyei

See `refugees.md` for the details.
- **Job:** incentive work with agencies in the camp; formal jobs usually need a Class M permit, which is slow and scarce.
- **Business:** services and tailoring for the camp, Kakuma town, and the host community.
- **Further education:** mostly through scholarships with yearly calls and heavy competition, plus camp-based training (JWL, Don Bosco, hub courses). Always plan a parallel earning or learning step while waiting.
- **Online work:** side income at best; ID verification and payment barriers are common.

## Changing course

Plans change. If a test fails or life shifts, record what was learned in the plan and compare again. Name what carries over: a CV built for a job search helps with college applications; costing skills from a business help with any budget; a trade test helps in both a job and a business.
