# Further Education and Training Pathway (Kenya)

Written October 2026. Kenya's education fees, intakes, and funding rules are changing fast. Use this file as a map, then **confirm current details with a web search** of official sites (kuccps.ac.ke, tveta.go.ke, tvetcdacc.go.ke, knec.ac.ke, nita.go.ke, knqa.go.ke, the institution's own site) or major Kenyan newspapers before anyone applies or pays. Items marked *(in transition)* are especially likely to have changed. For refugees, read `refugees.md` first: access, fees, and funding can differ.

## 1. How to coach this pathway

1. **Start from the goal, not the course.** "What work do you want this training to lead to?" A course is worth it only if it opens work they want, at pay that justifies the cost and time.
2. **Check what they can enter now** with their KCSE grade, certificates, or experience (section 2).
3. **Compare two or three routes** in the plan's "Courses I'm considering" table: level, institution and accreditation, entry requirement, length and mode (full-time, evening, online, weekend), total cost (fees, exams, fare, tools, *and* lost earnings), funding, next intake, and what it leads to.
4. **Test first:** visit or call the college, talk to current students and recent graduates, ask the institution which body certifies the course and what share of graduates find work, try a short or free module.
5. **Plan the money** (section 6) and the application steps with dates.
6. **Keep earning if possible.** Many people do best studying part-time, in evening classes, or in a later intake while working.

Use the assistance dial for choosing a course, applications, and fees plans. Never apply or pay on their behalf.

## 2. School systems and what a grade opens

**8-4-4 (old system):** KCSE on a 12-point scale, A (12) to E (1). Most adult users are from 8-4-4. **2027 is the last 8-4-4 KCSE sitting** *(in transition)*.
**CBC/CBE (new system):** senior school started January 2026; the first cohort finishes Grade 12 in 2028. These learners are mostly under 18 in 2026.

| KCSE mean grade | Typical next step |
|---|---|
| C+ and above | Degree (KNQF Level 7). Cluster subject requirements apply per course. |
| C- and above | Diploma (Level 6) at a TVET college or national polytechnic |
| D and above (some courses ask D+) | Craft certificate (Level 5) |
| E and above, or no KCSE | Artisan certificate (Level 4), Vocational Training Centres (VTCs), NITA trade tests |

Messages to give, honestly:
- **Every grade has a route forward.** For 2026 TVET placement, KUCCPS accepted any KCSE grade from 2000 to 2025.
- **TVET is a ladder:** artisan → craft certificate → diploma → degree (a diploma is an accepted entry route at some universities, including the Open University of Kenya).
- **Missing C+ is the common experience.** In the 2025 KCSE, about 271,000 of 993,000 candidates got C+ or above.
- Entry requirements differ by course and college. Check the specific course.

## 3. TVET: who does what, and how to apply

- **TVETA** accredits and licenses TVET institutions. **Always check that a college is accredited before paying.** In April 2026 TVETA revoked the accreditation of the Kenya Institute of Management and said its qualifications issued after 2018 aren't recognised, so accreditation matters.
- **Recognised certificates come from a recognised assessor** (TVET CDACC for competency-based courses, KNEC for older courses, NITA for trade tests). TVETA says private colleges' own certificates are "internal documents," not national qualifications. Always ask: "Who examines and certifies this course?"
- **KNEC** courses are being phased out in favour of CDACC competency-based courses by about November 2027 *(in transition)*. Prefer CDACC courses for new enrolment.
- **Where:** national polytechnics, technical and vocational colleges, and county VTCs (VTCs often accept people without KCSE; some counties have waived VTC tuition, e.g., Elgeyo Marakwet; check the county).

**Applying through KUCCPS (for government-sponsored places):**
- Applications open only in announced windows. In 2026 the main window ran 7 April–6 May, a TVET window closed 8 June, and a later TVET window closed 5 August. KUCCPS has described TVET applications as continuously open, but windows and dates vary, so check the portal or call KUCCPS. Public TVET colleges usually admit in January, May, and September, and many also take self-sponsored students directly; ask the college whether direct entry gets the same fee and HELB eligibility.
- Log in with the KCSE index number and year. Start at **www.kuccps.ac.ke** and follow its links (the student portal has been at students.kuccps.net).
- Application fee: KSh 1,500 on eCitizen in the 2026 main cycle (it was cut to KSh 500 for a 2025 TVET-only window). Keep the eCitizen reference number.
- Free help: KUCCPS 020 513 7400 or 0713 924 444, info@kuccps.ac.ke, any Huduma Centre, or the nearest TVET institution.
- People who never sat KCSE can't apply through KUCCPS. Their routes are VTCs and NITA trade tests, direct admission where a college allows it, Recognition of Prior Learning, or sitting KCSE (section 7).

**Fees *(in transition)*:** the government set a uniform public TVET fee of **KSh 67,189 a year (including assessment) from May 2026**. Colleges add charges (registration, ID, medical, welfare, often a few thousand shillings). KUCCPS-placed students in public TVETs can get government scholarship plus HELB loan, means-tested (section 6). Private colleges and self-sponsored places cost more and get less support.

## 4. Routes for computer-school graduates

**Recognised computer credentials, from first step up:**
| Option | What it is | Notes (October 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **ICDL** | International digital-literacy certification, accredited in Kenya | Mapped to KNQF **Level 3**: a good, portable proof of office skills, but below an artisan certificate. No national price; the Open University of Kenya sells ICDL Core and ICDL Professional at KSh 12,000 each, online and self-paced. Test centres include several universities and national polytechnics; verify the centre. |
| **ICT Authority Smart Academy** | Government digital-skills courses | Foundational about KSh 500, basic about KSh 2,500, intermediate units about KSh 5,000, plus free partner sessions (smartacademy.go.ke). |
| **Jitume hubs** | Government digital hubs (400+) | Free internet and digital-skills training with a national ID (refugee access unverified; ask the hub); online-work (Ajira) training where funded. Budgets were cut, so what's offered varies by hub. |
| **ICT Technician Level 5 (craft)** | CDACC competency-based | Entry roughly D (plain) and above; about 1–2 years including attachment, depending on the college. |
| **ICT / Computer Science Level 6 (diploma)** | CDACC competency-based | Entry C- (or Level 5 in a related area); about 18 months to 2 years plus attachment. Some evening classes. |
| **Degree** | KUCCPS placement (C+) | In 2026 the regular degree cycle mainly targeted 2025 KCSE candidates (check the current rules for earlier years); the **Open University of Kenya** took C+ holders from KCSE 2000–2025. |
| **Open University of Kenya (OUK)** | Online public university | ICT degrees (Computer Science, Data Science, Cyber Security, and others) about KSh 79,000 a year; entry by KCSE C+, a diploma, or recognition of prior learning (e.g., two years' work or two 3-month courses); short courses KSh 5,000; applications all year. KUCCPS-placed students can get scholarship and loan; self-sponsored students can apply to HELB. Contacts: 0702 212 212, admissions@ouk.ac.ke. Check device and data needs before enrolling. |
| **ALX Africa** | Free (sponsored) tech training | Ages 18–34, own laptop and stable internet, English; a one-off admin fee may apply. Online, demanding. |
| **Paid bootcamps** (e.g., Moringa) | Intensive coding and tech courses | Often KSh 40,000–200,000. Check outcomes before paying. |

Coaching points:
- Be honest about what each step leads to. ICDL plus a strong CV helps for office, data-entry, and customer-care jobs. A CDACC ICT certificate or diploma is the path to technical ICT work. Online completion certificates are not national qualifications.
- Employers in a 2025 skills survey rated digital skills (76%) and soft skills (83%) essential, and internships are becoming a main entry route. Pair training with real practice: attachment, volunteering, or a small portfolio.
- Don't promise that any programme guarantees a job or income.

## 5. Routes for sewing-school graduates

| Option | What it is | Notes (October 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **NITA Government Trade Test, Grade III** (dressmaking / garment making) | National trade certificate | Grade III is open to anyone; Grade II needs Grade III; Grade I needs Grade II. Fees on NITA's undated page: Grade III KSh 5,000, Grade II KSh 6,000, Grade I KSh 7,500. Test series run in April, August, and December (2026: 1–19 December); Grade III is mainly tested in the December series. Booking closes well before the test (the published window for the April series was 2 January–2 March), so check the deadline as soon as a trade test comes up; missing it can mean waiting a year. Book on NITA's form at a NITA booking outlet, or ask a nearby VTC or polytechnic. NITA: +254 20 2695586/9. |
| **Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)** | Certification of skills already learned | Through accredited assessment centres (portfolio, conversation, practical observation; about 6 weeks). Costs vary by units (TVET institutions reported about KSh 1,500–3,000). The national rollout has been slower than promised; for most graduates, the Grade III trade test is simpler. KNQA: rpl@knqa.go.ke. |
| **County VTC courses** | Fashion design and dressmaking | For example, a Nairobi county VTC runs a 12-month course (about KSh 45,000, minimum KCPE) leading to NITA trade tests. Fees vary by county; some waive tuition. |
| **Fashion Design and Garment Making, CDACC Levels 4–6** | TVET certificate and diploma | The Level 6 diploma needs KCSE C- (or Level 5 in a related area), about 2 years. Levels 4 and 5 have lower entry requirements; check each institution. |
| **NITA Textile Training Institute (N-TTI), Industrial Area, Nairobi** | Industry short courses | Sewing Machine Operator for the clothing industry (8 weeks + 3 months attachment), Multi-Skill Sewing Machine Operator (3 months; needs Grade III or a garment qualification), Sewing Machine Mechanic (3 months), Computerised Embroidery, **Bag and Fashion Accessories** (3 months), Dressmaking and Tailoring (9 months). Intakes January, May, September. About KSh 22,500 a term (day). Also a Diploma in Textile Technology. |
| **Generation Kenya Sewing Machine Operator** | 8-week factory-linked bootcamp | Ages 18–35, KCPE or higher; guarantees an interview (not a job) with partner factories. Check intakes and any cost. |
| **Business-skills training** | Pricing, records, growth | ILO Start and Improve Your Business (through certified trainers; ask locally), Hustler Fund's Tukuze Hub (by invitation to good borrowers). |

Coaching points:
- For a tailor running her own business, a **Grade III trade test** is the most practical credential: it helps with schools, institutions, tenders (AGPO), and factory jobs, and it costs little compared with a diploma.
- For factory work, a short operator course or the Generation programme may matter more than a diploma. There's no evidence factories require NITA certificates.
- A diploma suits someone aiming at design, teaching, or supervision who can manage two years of fees and time.
- Leather and bag making are growing skills (an ITC-supported leather bag course ran at Don Bosco in Nairobi in 2025; N-TTI has a bag course). Don't promise intakes; check.

## 6. Paying for it

- **HELB / Higher Education Financing (HEF) portal.** For university and TVET students with an admission letter. **Refugees:** HELB requires a Kenyan national ID; see `refugees.md` for scholarships. The 2026/27 first-time window opened 10 July 2026; applying is free. Needs an admission letter, a national ID, KCPE and KCSE index numbers and years, parents' IDs and phone numbers, guarantors (parents may count), and, per 2026 reports, a phone line registered in the student's own name. Someone on a shared phone should get a SIM in their own name first. Means testing puts students in five bands (orphans, registered persons with disabilities, and Inua Jamii beneficiaries go to the neediest band); appeals are possible with evidence.
- **The new funding model and "free tertiary education" *(in transition)*.** Courts have gone back and forth on the 2023 banded model, which currently applies. In July 2026 the President pledged full funding for placed students, through a bill to merge HELB and the funds into one Tertiary Education Funding Authority, with means testing kept and the start pushed to about January 2027. **Don't tell anyone college is free now.**
- **NG-CDF bursaries** (constituency) and **county bursaries**, which often include TVET students; each office sets its own window and form (usually needs a fee structure, admission letter, and chief's verification).
- **Saving toward fees:** a merry-go-round turn timed to the fees deadline, a savings group, or saving from a job (see `money-and-groups.md`).
- **Scholarships:** check carefully. Many are for specific groups (for example, Wings to Fly TVET scholarships are for its own alumni). Never pay to apply for a scholarship.
- **Total cost, not just tuition:** fees, exams, registration, tools or materials, fare, rent if moving, data, and income given up. Build it in the plan.

## 7. Second chances

- **Re-sitting KCSE as a private candidate:** KSh 7,200 (2026), registered through the Sub-County Director of Education; adults over 24 register as private candidates. **2027 is the last 8-4-4 sitting**; watch for the registration window. **Important:** sitting only a few subjects gives a supplementary slip that **does not change the mean grade**. To change a mean grade, a full re-sit (7–9 subjects) is needed. Weigh this against the TVET ladder, which is often faster.
- **No KCPE:** KNEC ran a KCSE Qualifying Test in 2026; ask KNEC or the Sub-County Education office whether another sitting is planned.
- **Recognition of Prior Learning** for skills learned informally (section 5); for ICT it's mostly at basic levels.

## 8. Attachment, apprenticeships, and internships

- **Industrial attachment** is part of most TVET courses, arranged through the institution and NITA's ITAP portal. Help them draft attachment request letters to local employers.
- **NITA apprenticeship and dual training** (about 60% workplace, 40% institution) exists for some trades; check what's offered locally.
- **Public Service Internship Programme:** still for degree holders (graduated 2020 or later), KSh 25,000 a month for 12 months; opening it to diploma and TVET graduates is under review only.
- **County internships:** check the County Public Service Board website.

## 9. Education scams and pitfalls

- **Unaccredited colleges** and "internal certificates." Check TVETA (TVET) or the Commission for University Education (universities) before paying.
- **Fake KUCCPS and HELB "services":** fake transfer or placement links charging fees. KUCCPS's only fee is paid on eCitizen; HELB applications are free. No one can sell a placement, a loan, or a scholarship.
- **Offers passed on by relatives or friends** ("a connection at KUCCPS"). Assume the person means well. Help them check directly with KUCCPS (0713 924 444) and offer to draft a respectful reply. Never let anyone else log in to their KUCCPS or eCitizen account or pay on their behalf. Keep the relative's name out of a plan on a shared phone.
- **"Guaranteed" scholarships** offered on social media for a "processing fee" or an ID photo are always fraud.
- **Buying certificates** is fraud and can end a career. Never.
- **Overseas study or work-study offers** that ask for money up front or a passport. Check the institution and any recruitment agency (NEAIMS for work).
- **Online "certificates"** that employers don't recognise. Useful for learning; be honest that they aren't qualifications.

## 10. Where to get human help

The nearest national polytechnic or TVET college (admissions office), the county VTC, Huduma Centres (KUCCPS help), the Sub-County Director of Education (KCSE registration, bursaries), NITA offices (trade tests), constituency NG-CDF offices (bursaries), Jitume hubs, and their PFC trainer. Offer to draft their questions.
