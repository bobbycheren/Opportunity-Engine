# Official Requirements and Verification Rules (Kenya)

## The verification rule

Fees, permits, tax rules, and deadlines change, and county rules differ. **Never state them from memory as current.**

1. Use web search to find the current answer on an official source (.go.ke sites, KRA, BRS, the county government, MSEA, SHA, NSSF) or a major Kenyan newspaper reporting an official announcement.
2. Tell them the source and when you checked it: "According to KRA's Turnover Tax page, checked today..."
3. Separate clearly: (a) verified requirements, (b) general business advice, (c) calculations from their numbers, and (d) your suggestions.
4. Tell them to confirm with the office before paying or applying, and to pay only through official channels (eCitizen, the county's official system, KRA) and keep the receipt.
5. If you can't verify something, say so and give them the office to ask instead of guessing.
6. If web search isn't available, say so, avoid specific figures, and point them to the sources below.

Figures below were checked in October 2026 and are a starting point, not the answer.

## What a very small tailor usually needs

For someone sewing for customers and earning, say, KSh 15,000–40,000 a month in sales:

| Step | Needed? | Notes (October 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **KRA PIN** | Often asked for (tills, banks, tenders, some funds); not needed just to start sewing from home | Apply on iTax (itax.kra.go.ke) with a national ID; widely understood to be free. Many offices, banks, tills, and tenders ask for it. Once they earn business income, the PIN should carry an income tax obligation. |
| **County business permit** | Usually, if they trade from a fixed place (stall, shop, workshop) | Issued by the county government, renewed yearly (in Turkana, every January). Fees vary hugely by county, zone, and business size, from around KSh 1,000 a year for small informal traders in some counties to much more in Nairobi. Look up the county's current Finance Act or ask the county revenue office. Working from home or as a hawker has different rules; ask. NYOTA beneficiaries were promised a two-year permit waiver; check whether it is in force. |
| **SHA (health insurance)** (a personal obligation, not a business licence) | Registration is compulsory for everyone | Register by dialling *147#, online, or at a Huduma Centre. Informal earners pay 2.75% of household income as assessed by SHA's means test, with a KSh 300 a month minimum; "Lipa SHA Pole Pole" allows instalments. Cover depends on being paid up. |
| **NSSF Haba Haba** | Optional | Voluntary pension savings for self-employed people; small daily, weekly, or monthly amounts. Check the current USSD code and minimum on nssf.or.ke. |
| **Business name registration (BRS)** | Only if trading under a name other than their own | Trading as "Grace Wanjiku" needs no registration; "Grace Wanjiku Tailoring" or "Neema Designs" does, within 28 days of starting to use it. On eCitizen (brs.go.ke): about KSh 950 plus a small eCitizen fee and a name search. Needed for AGPO, some bank accounts, and tills. |
| **Limited company** | Almost never at the start | About KSh 10,650 plus ongoing annual filings and obligations. Refer to a professional if they're considering it. |
| **Turnover Tax** | Not below KSh 1 million a year in sales | 1.5% of gross sales for turnover between KSh 1 million and KSh 25 million a year (KRA). Below KSh 1 million, business profit is declared as ordinary income in the annual return. From 1 January 2027, individual returns are due by 30 April (Finance Act 2026, per KRA). |
| **VAT** | No, below KSh 5 million a year | Proposals to change the threshold have been floated; check. |
| **eTIMS (electronic invoices)** | Rules are changing | The law requires businesses to issue electronic tax invoices, but sales by "small businesses" (turnover KSh 5 million or less) to other businesses can be invoiced by the buyer instead, which the seller approves on eCitizen or *222#. Expenses without eTIMS invoices can't be deducted. KRA has asked Parliament to change these rules. Check KRA's current eTIMS guidance before advising. |
| **Data protection** | Basic duty only | Keeping customers' names, phone numbers, and measurements is personal data: keep it private and use it only for their orders. Very small businesses are exempt from registering with the Data Commissioner. |

Teach the habit, not the law: keep a record book, keep receipts, separate business money, and ask KRA or a Huduma Centre when earnings grow.

## Groups

- **Self-help group registration:** State Department for Social Protection, at the sub-county social development office or a Huduma Centre. As of October 2026: KSh 1,000 for a group, KSh 5,000 for a community-based organisation (CBO). Needs a name search, minutes electing officials, a signed member list with IDs, and a constitution; annual renewal after the first two years. The law behind it is in transition (see `money-and-groups.md`).
- **Cooperatives:** at least 10 members; register through the county cooperative office. New SACCO registration has strict conditions that small groups can't meet.
- **MSEA associations:** the Micro and Small Enterprises Authority registers associations of small businesses and offers worksites, shared machinery, training, and market links. A group of tailors may benefit; check msea.go.ke or the county MSEA office.

## AGPO (government tenders reserved for youth, women, and persons with disabilities)

- A share of government procurement is reserved for these groups (the law sets 30%).
- Eligibility: a registered business (sole proprietorship with a business name, partnership, or company) at least 70% owned and fully led by youth (up to 34 or 35; sources differ), women, or persons with disabilities. Self-help groups and cooperatives are not listed as eligible.
- Documents: ID, business registration certificate, KRA PIN, a tax compliance certificate, and for persons with disabilities an NCPWD document.
- Apply at agpo.go.ke or with help at a Huduma Centre. Check for any official fee there. No one can sell a tender.
- See `tailoring-business.md` for whether tenders make sense for a small tailor.

## Pitfalls

- Paid "agents" who charge large fees for things eCitizen, KRA, or the county do directly.
- Fake permits and receipts: pay the county through its official system and keep the official receipt.
- Fake officials demanding cash on the spot. Ask for identification and an official receipt; if unsure, ask the county office.
